Selected Work
Paradromics
Clinical-stage brain-computer interface company
A clinical-stage neurotechnology company needed credibility in a market dominated by consumer tech hype.
Context

Paradromics is developing a fully implantable brain-computer interface for patients with ALS and locked-in syndrome, requiring FDA clearance, payer reimbursement coverage, and clinical adoption to reach the market. The company competed in a category defined by Neuralink's consumer-facing media presence, where clinicians and biotech investors required clinical evidence to take a company seriously. Patients presented an additional barrier: fear of invasiveness and loss of agency from a brain-implanted device. AI search engines were surfacing competitors ahead of Paradromics for key queries, and the company had no distinct position within the clinical BCI category.

Solution

Developed the "Adult in the Room" brand strategy, anchoring the Paradromics identity in clinical evidence and calm conviction, with brand voice guidelines built around peer-reviewed proof points including the SONIC benchmark, and an Embodiment messaging framework that repositioned the device as something the patient partners with rather than something implanted in them. Built an HCP messaging house for neurologist outreach covering the core value proposition, scientific credibility, anticipated objections, and tone guidance, including a dedicated pillar addressing the concern that a referral would transfer longitudinal care responsibility, alongside clinical trial recruitment materials giving care coordinators specific patient scenarios and language for identifying eligible participants. Built the AEO/GEO strategy that resulted in Paradromics being described as the "leading developer" and "premier high-bandwidth neurotechnology leader" in AI-generated responses, supported by keyword-rich content targeting biotech investors and medical device researchers. The work drove a 215% increase in web traffic and a 362% increase in social engagement.

Blue Cross Blue Shield of Alabama
Health insurance payer
Consumers in economic uncertainty defaulted to transactional thinking about health insurance.
Context

Blue Cross Blue Shield of Alabama faced a perception challenge where core managed care mechanisms, such as narrow networks, prior authorizations, and cost-sharing, were viewed as barriers rather than benefits, eroding engagement and trust in an already transactional category. The commercial segment added a B2B layer: employers, HR departments, brokers, and benefits consultants all influence the buying decision in a sales cycle that resembles enterprise software in length and stakeholder complexity. A single brand platform was needed that could speak to all three commercial audiences — Medicare Advantage members, small business group plans, and young adults entering the workforce — without flattening the distinctions between them.

Solution

Contributed to the We Cover What Matters and "The Caring Company" brand platforms, built to reframe managed care friction as evidence of coordinated, attentive coverage rather than bureaucratic restriction. The campaign used a transitions-of-care nurse as a social proof asset in creative, positioning care coordination as a member benefit rather than a cost-control mechanism. Demand capture included interactive banner ads functioning as live symptom checkers and doctor-finder tools, capturing high-intent users directly within the ad unit. Post-enrollment retention messaging surfaced member benefits to drive plan tool usage and reduce churn. The B2B and broker track required coordinating demand generation, sales enablement, and product marketing in sequence, equipping the sales force with third-party proof points to close group plan accounts with employers evaluating the BCBS card as an employee retention asset. The campaign delivered a Net Promoter Score three to four times the national average, making Blue Cross Blue Shield of Alabama the top-ranked Blue Cross plan in the country.

Moffitt Cancer Center
NCI-designated Comprehensive Cancer Center
Moffitt treated only 5% of Florida cancer patients despite clinical outcomes far above average.
Context

Moffitt Cancer Center is a freestanding NCI-designated Comprehensive Cancer Center in Tampa, Florida, operating without the built-in primary care referral networks that most academic medical systems depend on. Despite posting survival rates up to four times the national average for certain cancers, Moffitt was treating approximately 5% of Florida's cancer patients. A "Florida Stigma" caused affluent patients to default to legacy institutions in New York and Boston, and Moffitt was perceived as a last resort rather than a first call, with nearly 30% of patients arriving from other facilities with an incorrect diagnosis or staging. Research showed that 60 to 66% of physicians are very unlikely to change referral patterns without active, ongoing liaison engagement, making the physician sales enablement function a commercial necessity. A new CEO brought a mandate for bolder brand positioning across symptomatic patients, prevention audiences, researchers, and major donors.

Solution

Contributed to a brand positioning strategy anchored in Urgency, chosen specifically to align with the new CEO's vision and to counter the behavioral tendency toward complacency and deferred care that defined the "last resort" perception. Built distinct messaging tracks across four commercial audiences. For symptomatic patients, messaging interrupted the default toward out-of-state legacy institutions by reframing clinical decision-making as time-sensitive. For asymptomatic populations, Health Risk Assessment campaigns served as conversion gates, capturing patient data into CRM nurture streams to drive preventive screenings. For Medicare Advantage audiences, consumer-facing demand was engineered to create commercial pressure on private plans to keep Moffitt in-network. Developed a physician liaison sales enablement suite including referral gap kits that used patient volume data to show independent oncologists exactly where their patients were going instead of Moffitt, and competitive battlecards positioning Moffitt's survival rate data against out-of-state legacy institutions. The campaign delivered a 66.5% year-over-year increase in new patient conversions and a 40% lift in digital engagement.

OhioHealth Cancer
Community health system cancer program
A community health system was invisible in cancer care against a dominant academic competitor.
Context

OhioHealth is a community-based health system in Columbus, Ohio, competing in a cancer care market where Ohio State University's James Cancer Hospital held commanding brand recognition as an academic medical center with built-in research credibility. At the time of the engagement, 52% of OhioHealth's own associates and 83% of surveyed consumers named the James first when thinking about cancer care. The system was simultaneously launching a new Blood and Marrow Transplant inpatient unit, requiring a campaign that could build category credibility while managing the psychological and insurance barriers specific to cancer care in a community health system setting.

Solution

Contributed to the Keep Making Plans campaign, positioning OhioHealth's cancer program around life after treatment and adopting a challenger brand stance by leading with emotional impact over institutional credential. Experiential activations met audiences in familiar community settings, reducing the psychological barriers that often deter patients from seeking specialist care at a community health system. To support the launch of the Blood and Marrow Transplant unit, developed a physician liaison sales enablement suite including referral gap kits that used patient volume data to show independent oncologists exactly where they were sending transplant patients instead of OhioHealth, and competitive battlecards that gave liaisons a direct clinical and emotional narrative for closing referral gaps against the James. The "Thrivership" narrative anchored the payer and employer track, framing OhioHealth's cancer outcomes in terms of returning working-age adults to their functional daily lives, giving payers a cost-efficiency argument alongside the clinical one.

Organogenesis
Regenerative medicine and medical devices
Patients with chronic joint pain avoided treatment because they feared surgery and doubted options.
Context

Organogenesis's ReNu is a single-injection amniotic suspension allograft administered by orthopedic specialists to provide long-lasting relief for osteoarthritis and joint pain. As a novel biologic treatment, ReNu faced significant healthcare system barriers including coverage restrictions, prior authorization requirements, and step therapy protocols from insurance intermediaries who classified it as experimental. Patients presented an additional behavioral barrier, frequently reporting joint pain but failing to seek treatment when they associated medical intervention with surgical complexity or short-term results.

Solution

Contributed to a campaign that applied System 1 behavioral framing to position ReNu as the intuitive, low-risk path to long-lasting relief, shifting the patient decision away from deliberate clinical evaluation and toward fast, instinctive action. Because insurance intermediaries used prior authorizations and formulary restrictions to classify ReNu as experimental, demand generation focused on a high-intent audience of affluent osteoarthritis patients with household incomes above $100,000 who were willing to pay out-of-pocket, bypassing the payer gatekeeping problem through audience selection rather than coverage negotiation. The content strategy mapped demand across the full conversion path: social awareness and physician FAQ content captured research-phase interest, while retargeting sequences drove direct calls to action. The Physician Finder tool functioned as the primary sales enablement mechanism, routing motivated patients to orthopedic specialists who had been pre-educated on the ReNu protocol, so that by the time a patient arrived, the physician was already prepared to administer the treatment rather than evaluate it.
